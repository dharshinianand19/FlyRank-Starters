1. Introduction

This project investigates whether historical search-performance metrics can help identify content items that may experience a significant decline in future search impressions.

The objective is to support FlyRank editors in prioritizing content for investigation using historical impressions, clicks, average search position, and reporting-data availability.

The model provides a decision-support signal. It does not automatically decide which pages should be refreshed.

2. Dataset and Preparation

The project uses the FlyRank Internship — Pseudonymized Warehouse Release (v20260703), a frozen warehouse snapshot exported on July 3, 2026.

Dataset repository: FlyRank/internship-warehouse

The analysis uses:

  • dim_content for schema inspection and content metadata.
  • fact_content_daily_performance for daily search-performance metrics.

Monthly performance partitions from March through June 2026 were aggregated by pseudonymous client-content pair. Records required at least 15 days of available Google Search Console data per month. Records with zero current-month average daily impressions were excluded from target construction.

Matched content-month records: 338,853

Pseudonymous identifiers were used for matching records only, not as model features. Future outcomes and label-derived fields were excluded from the predictive inputs.

3. Methodology

Research question

Can current-month search-performance metrics identify content items that experience at least a 20% decline in average daily impressions in the following month?

Target definition

A record is labelled as a decline when next-month average daily impressions are less than or equal to 80% of current-month average daily impressions.

  • 1: Decline of at least 20%.
  • 0: Decline of less than 20%, no decline, or an increase.

Model features

  1. current_impressions
  2. current_clicks
  3. current_position
  4. current_available_days

Model

Logistic Regression was implemented using a Scikit-learn pipeline with median imputation, feature standardization, and Logistic Regression. The model uses class weighting, max_iter=500, random_state=42, and a classification threshold of 0.5.

4. Baseline Comparison

Two simple baselines were evaluated against the model.

  1. Majority-class baseline: predicts the training-set majority class for every test record.
  2. Low-impressions rule: predicts decline when current impressions are below the median current impressions in the training data.

7. Ranked Recommendations

The ranked output orders test records by predicted probability of next-month decline. The following tiers are capacity-based editorial review recommendations, not validated probability thresholds.

Priority 1: High-priority review

Top 10% of ranked records.

  • Inspect impressions, clicks, and average-position changes.
  • Check whether information is outdated.
  • Review search-intent alignment before considering a refresh.

Priority 2: Investigate

Next 20% of ranked records.

  • Verify reporting-data availability.
  • Monitor performance changes.
  • Review possible relevance or freshness issues.

Priority 3: Monitor

Remaining 70% of ranked records.

  • Continue routine monthly monitoring.
  • Avoid unnecessary content changes.
  • Reassess when new performance data becomes available.

Editors should verify the underlying evidence before taking action. Predictions are prioritization signals, not automatic refresh instructions.

8. Limitations and Proposed Solutions

  1. Moderate predictive discrimination: ROC-AUC is 0.6004. Benchmark Random Forest and Gradient Boosting on the same temporal split.
  2. Limited evaluation period: Only one test feature month was evaluated. Use rolling time-based validation across multiple months.
  3. Repeated-content overlap: The same content may occur in training and testing. Add content-grouped evaluation and, where relevant, held-out-client evaluation.
  4. Search-data availability: The 15-day reporting requirement excludes some low-volume or unindexed items. Evaluate performance across availability tiers and do not treat missing data as zero.
  5. Narrow target definition: A 20% impressions decline does not prove content decay or show that refreshing content will help. Use editorial review and search-intent checks.
  6. Fixed threshold and calibration: A 0.5 threshold may not suit editorial capacity, and class weighting may affect probability calibration. Evaluate thresholds and calibration on validation data.
  7. Limited feature coverage: The model uses four performance features. Test additional leak-free historical and content features on validation data before including them.
